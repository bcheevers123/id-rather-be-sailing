You are a senior full-stack engineer, data engineer, web-scraping specialist, product designer and technical architect.

Your task is to research, design and implement a production-quality web application that helps professional seafarers find MCA-approved maritime training courses, the approved centres offering them, and upcoming course dates and prices where that information is publicly available.

## 1. Project context

This application is being created for my brother and his colleagues. My brother is a deputy captain on a superyacht and regularly needs to find maritime training courses required for his role and career progression.

I am developing the application, but I know very little about sailing, maritime qualifications, MCA terminology or the relationships between the different courses and certificates.

Therefore:

* Do not assume I understand maritime terminology.
* Explain important maritime concepts in plain English.
* Define acronyms when first used.
* Do not rely on me to identify whether two similarly named courses are equivalent.
* Validate course names and classifications against authoritative sources.
* Ask targeted questions only when the answer materially affects the product or architecture.
* Do not ask me to repeat requirements already provided below.

Use UK English throughout.

## 2. Primary authoritative source

The initial authoritative source for MCA-approved courses and training providers is:

https://www.gov.uk/guidance/mca-approved-training-providers-atp

Inspect the complete page and every relevant linked document.

Do not assume that web scraping is automatically the correct solution. First investigate whether GOV.UK, the Maritime and Coastguard Agency, individual providers or another authoritative source exposes:

* Structured HTML
* JSON data
* APIs
* CSV or spreadsheet downloads
* Document metadata
* Stable PDF URLs
* RSS or update feeds
* Sitemap data
* Other machine-readable sources

Prefer stable, structured and authorised data sources over brittle HTML scraping.

The MCA source should determine:

* Which courses exist
* The official course name
* The course category
* Which providers are approved for each course
* The provider’s location and contact details
* The source document establishing the approval
* When the official source was last updated

The official MCA list establishes approval status. It does not necessarily prove that a provider is currently scheduling the course.

## 3. Core user need

A user must be able to:

1. Browse all available MCA-listed courses.
2. Search for a course by its full name, partial name, abbreviation or common alternative name.
3. Select a course.
4. See every approved training centre associated with that course.
5. See upcoming dates offered by each centre where reliable dates can be found.
6. See prices where reliable public pricing can be found.
7. Open the relevant provider booking or course page.
8. Filter and sort the results.
9. View upcoming courses in a rolling calendar.

The app must not require users to create an account.

There are no saved searches, favourites, alerts, email notifications or personalised user data in the MVP.

## 4. Required application views

### Course catalogue

Create a browseable catalogue containing every course found through the approved MCA source.

Users should be able to browse by logical category, such as:

* STCW basic training
* STCW advanced training
* Refresher or updating training
* Tanker training
* Security training
* Deck yacht modules
* Engineering modules
* Radio and communications training
* Other MCA-approved training

Use the official classifications where possible. Do not invent categories that could mislead users.

Each course entry should include:

* Official course name
* Common abbreviation, where verified
* Brief plain-English explanation
* Category
* Number of approved centres
* Earliest known upcoming date
* Lowest known public price
* Data freshness indicator

Do not generate course descriptions from guesswork. Clearly distinguish authoritative descriptions from concise interface summaries.

### Course results page

After choosing a course, show the approved providers associated with it.

Each provider result should display, where available:

* Provider name
* Town or city
* Region
* Country
* Full address
* Distance from a chosen location, if implemented reliably
* Upcoming course dates
* Course duration
* Price
* Currency
* Tax or VAT information when explicitly stated
* Delivery format, such as in-person, blended or online
* Availability or booking status, if explicitly published
* Direct course or booking link
* Provider website
* Telephone number
* Email address
* MCA approval source
* Date the approval source was last updated
* Date the provider information was last checked
* Data freshness or stale-data warning

A provider must remain visible even when no dates or prices can be found. In that situation, show clear text such as:

* “No public dates found”
* “Price not published”
* “Contact provider”
* “Schedule information could not be verified”

Never invent, estimate or infer a date, price, duration or availability status.

### Rolling calendar

Provide a calendar view showing known upcoming course instances.

The calendar should:

* Default to a useful rolling period, such as the next three or six months.
* Support moving forwards and backwards through time.
* Allow filtering by course, provider and location.
* Clearly display multi-day courses.
* Handle different countries, currencies and time zones.
* Link each event to its provider and booking source.
* Exclude undated offerings rather than assigning artificial dates.
* Work well on desktop and mobile.

Consider offering both:

* A conventional month calendar.
* A chronological agenda or timeline view.

The agenda view may be more practical on mobile.

## 5. Filters and sorting

At minimum, support filtering by:

* Course
* Course category
* Country
* Region
* Town or city
* Date range
* Provider
* Maximum price
* Currency
* Delivery format, where known
* Whether an upcoming date is available
* Whether a public price is available

Support sorting by:

* Earliest upcoming date
* Lowest price
* Location
* Provider name
* Most recently verified

Investigate whether distance-based filtering can be implemented accurately without introducing paid services or unnecessary infrastructure.

Do not combine prices from different currencies into a misleading comparison. Currency conversion should only be added if a reliable approach and clear conversion timestamp are provided.

## 6. Search behaviour

The search should tolerate:

* Partial course names
* Common abbreviations
* Punctuation differences
* Minor spelling errors
* Alternate spacing
* Terms such as “refresher”, “updated” or “updating”
* Similar courses that must remain distinct

Create a controlled alias system rather than relying entirely on fuzzy search.

For example, the application must not accidentally merge:

* An initial course with its refresher course
* Operational and management-level versions
* Yacht-restricted and unrestricted versions
* Basic and advanced qualifications

Where two courses are easily confused, show a brief clarification.

## 7. Data acquisition and refresh strategy

There are two distinct data layers.

### Layer A: official approval data

Source this from the MCA/GOV.UK material.

This layer establishes:

* Course identity
* Official course category
* Approved provider
* Provider contact information
* Approval source
* Official-source update date

### Layer B: provider offering data

Source this from the approved provider’s own website where permitted.

This layer may establish:

* Upcoming dates
* Price
* Currency
* Course duration
* Delivery format
* Availability
* Booking URL

Never treat a third-party intermediary as the actual approved training provider unless the relationship is clearly identified.

For every field, retain provenance so that the application can identify:

* The source URL
* The time it was retrieved
* The parser or adapter used
* Whether it came from an official MCA source or provider source
* Whether the data was directly stated or normalised
* Whether the source is now stale or unavailable

## 8. Scraping and responsible data collection

Before implementing a scraper for any site:

* Check for structured data or an API.
* Review robots.txt where applicable.
* Review relevant terms and usage restrictions.
* Use a descriptive user agent.
* Apply conservative rate limiting.
* Cache responses.
* Avoid repeatedly downloading unchanged files.
* Use conditional requests where supported.
* Do not bypass authentication, CAPTCHAs, anti-bot measures or access controls.
* Do not scrape private, logged-in or non-public information.
* Do not attempt to disguise automated activity.

If a provider does not permit reliable automated collection:

* Keep the provider in the approved-centre results.
* Link to its website.
* Mark dates and prices as unavailable.
* Record the reason in the internal coverage report.

Use provider-specific adapters where necessary rather than creating one fragile universal parser.

If multiple providers use a common booking platform, investigate whether a reusable adapter is possible.

## 9. Data quality and freshness

The app must communicate data quality honestly.

For each course offering, support statuses such as:

* Verified
* Recently checked
* Stale
* Source unavailable
* Parsing failed
* No public schedule
* No public price

Choose sensible freshness thresholds based on the type of data.

For example:

* Approval data may follow the official MCA update cycle.
* Course dates and prices may need more frequent checks.
* Past course dates must be removed or archived automatically.
* A failed refresh should not silently replace valid data with empty data.

Retain the last known valid result when a temporary source failure occurs, but visibly mark it as stale.

Implement change detection so that significant changes can be reviewed, including:

* A provider being added or removed
* A course list changing
* A provider URL changing
* A parser suddenly returning no courses
* A major price change
* Previously available dates disappearing
* A source document changing structure

Generate machine-readable refresh logs and a human-readable coverage report.

## 10. Stateless versus stateful architecture

The user-facing MVP does not require accounts or stored user preferences.

Evaluate at least these approaches:

### Option A: fully static generated application

A scheduled data pipeline collects and normalises data, generates static JSON, and publishes the frontend and data to GitHub Pages.

### Option B: static frontend with serverless data jobs or endpoints

The frontend remains static, while scheduled collection or data access uses a low-cost serverless platform.

### Option C: application with a database and backend

A backend stores provider, course, offering and refresh history data.

For each option, compare:

* Hosting cost
* Maintenance burden
* Reliability
* Data freshness
* Scraping compatibility
* GitHub Pages compatibility
* Scheduled-job support
* Observability
* Scalability
* Failure recovery
* Security
* Risk of vendor lock-in

Prefer the simplest architecture that can reliably satisfy the requirements.

A particularly important possibility to investigate is a stateless user-facing application backed by pre-generated JSON that is refreshed through scheduled repository automation. This could preserve GitHub Pages hosting without performing scraping in the browser.

However, do not select this architecture without validating that it can support:

* Scheduled refreshes
* PDF parsing
* Provider-site collection
* Data generation
* Failure reporting
* Safe publication
* Reasonable execution limits

Verify current platform pricing and limitations rather than relying on outdated assumptions.

## 12. User experience and visual design

The interface should feel professional, trustworthy and efficient.

The intended users are working maritime professionals who may be:

* Searching quickly between contracts
* Travelling internationally
* Using a mobile phone
* Comparing dates across several countries
* Unfamiliar with which centre offers the next suitable course

Design principles:

* Mobile-first
* Fast loading
* Clear search and filters
* Minimal unnecessary navigation
* Accessible
* No account wall
* No decorative nautical clichés
* Clear source attribution
* Clear “last checked” information
* Easy access to the provider’s booking page

Aim for WCAG 2.2 AA accessibility.

Use semantic HTML, keyboard-accessible controls, visible focus states and appropriate screen-reader labels.

Filter state may be represented in the URL so searches can be shared or bookmarked without creating user accounts or persistent profiles.

## 13. Coverage expectations

The product must browse every course found in the official MCA source.

However, do not falsely claim complete coverage of dates and prices until it has been demonstrated.

Maintain a coverage report containing:

* Total official courses
* Total approved providers
* Total course-provider approval relationships
* Providers with working schedule extraction
* Providers with working price extraction
* Providers requiring manual review
* Providers that prohibit or block automated collection
* Providers without public schedules
* Last successful full refresh
* Current parser failures

The interface should explain that approval coverage and live schedule coverage are different.

## 15. Security and privacy

The MVP should collect no personal user data.

Avoid cookies unless genuinely necessary.

Do not introduce authentication.

Validate all generated data before rendering it.

Treat scraped content as untrusted input.

Prevent:

* Script injection
* Unsafe HTML rendering
* Malicious source URLs
* Spreadsheet formula injection in exports
* Corrupt generated JSON
* Accidental publication of secrets
* Leakage of scraping credentials or tokens

Use repository secrets for any required credentials. Do not commit them.

Add provider adapters systematically.

* Do not fabricate prices.
